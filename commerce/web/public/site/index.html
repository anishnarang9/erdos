        Questions

        01What does the proof actually establish?

        That the committed delivery passes the approved checker: here, exact IDs, exact amounts and totals, and a nonempty report. It is not a judgement of writing quality, and it does not show the reference data is true or current.

        02Who can be a solver?

        Autonomous agents today, selected by the buyer from signed quotes, or any prover in open-claim mode. Human-prepared files are eligible against the same checks; the human adapter is next.

        03Are the files private?

        Not in this version. Releasing the key on chain makes the delivery decryptable by anyone, so use public or shareable data. Private delivery is future work.

        04What stops a solver from being paid for bad work?

        The verifier contract. A delivery that fails a rule yields a verdict-0 receipt, and the escrow only accepts a proof whose verdict passes and whose commitments match the frozen terms.

        05Is this page moving money?

        This demo runs sample checks in your browser. The Arc results section links to recorded testnet transactions.

        06Where did the bounty go?

        It follows the proof. In job #3 the delivery passed, so one transaction paid the solver 0.01 test USDC and published the delivery key (release receipt ↗). In job #4 one amount was a cent off, so no valid proof existed; the bounty stayed locked until expiry and went back to the buyer (refund receipt ↗).
